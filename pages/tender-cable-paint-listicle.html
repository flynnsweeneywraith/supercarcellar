The case, in five parts

1

The cable rests where you would never rest a tool

Nobody lays a spanner on a front guard. Yet a stored car spends whole seasons with a charger cable draped over the same edge of bodywork, pressing on the same square inch of clear coat the entire time.

2

Micro-movement does the damage

The cable does not sit still. Airflow from the garage door, footsteps on a timber floor, the small shift every time you check the charge. Each movement drags an unpadded cable across paint, and swirl marks are the receipt.

3

The towel fix fails at exactly the wrong time

Search any Corvette, Porsche or Ferrari forum and the thread ends the same way: someone drapes a microfibre towel under the cable and hopes. Towels slip. They slip most when the cable is moved, which is precisely when protection matters.

4

Correction costs more than prevention

A single stage of paint correction on a modern clear coat is a specialist, per-panel expense. A sleeve that stops the marks from forming costs less than the detailer's call-out fee.

5

The fix takes less time than folding the towel
